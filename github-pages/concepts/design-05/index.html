01 / The treatment

Small details.
Thoughtful attention.

Electrolysis uses a fine probe and a controlled electrical current to treat individual hair follicles. It works independently of hair pigment, so light, gray, and red hairs can be treated too.

01

Face & neck

Upper lip, chin, jawline, cheeks, sideburns, brows, hairline, and neck. Focus on a few persistent hairs or discuss a wider area.

The method in a moment

One follicle at a time.
A plan over time.

01 —

Target the follicle

A very fine probe follows the follicle’s natural opening. Controlled current treats the cells involved in hair growth.

02 —

Remove the treated hair

The treated hair is gently removed. Technique and settings reflect the area, hair, and skin response.

03 —

Return as growth appears

Hair grows in cycles. A series of visits allows treatment of hairs as they become available, with the plan reviewed along the way.

Good questions welcome

Let’s clear
a few things up.

Does hair color or skin tone matter?+

Electrolysis treats the follicle with electrical current rather than targeting pigment. It can treat all natural hair colors, including blonde, red, gray, and white, across skin tones. Your consultation helps establish individual suitability and settings.

Is it one appointment or a series?+

Plan for a series. Hair grows in cycles, and the visible hairs are only part of the picture. The area, hair density, previous removal methods, hormonal influences, and appointment consistency affect the schedule. There is no single treatment count or completion date for everyone.

What does electrolysis feel like?+

You may feel a brief warmth, pinch, or sting. Sensation depends on the person, area, and settings. A small test treatment, when appropriate, gives you a chance to experience it and discuss comfort before planning longer sessions.

What about hair linked to hormonal changes?+

Existing unwanted hair can be treated, including hair associated with PCOS or menopause. Electrolysis does not diagnose or treat the underlying condition, and hormonal changes can influence new growth. Discuss medical questions with your healthcare professional.
